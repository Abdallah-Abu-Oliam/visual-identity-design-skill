Foundations · three

The name supplied the colour.

The brief demanded warmth. Both obvious warm colours were already occupied — amber by the anti-nuclear campaign, saturated yellow by Orano.

Where the palette came from. A noyau is the stone of a stone fruit, and the fruit has a colour nobody in either category holds. Apricot was then pushed deliberately pinker — #E08A52 → #DE8560 — purely to widen the measured distance from Réseau Sortir du Nucléaire's amber from 90 to 105. That is differentiation being spent at the moment the primary is decided, which is where it belongs.

The PMS column is unverified. Pantone cannot be matched on a screen — the chips are printed ink under controlled light. Take a proof to a physical guide under D50 and pick by eye; whatever the printer picks there replaces these values. They are included rather than omitted because PMS is the code that gets dropped, and then packaging arrives wrong.

Layer order — 60 / 30 / 10 made operational

LayerColourRule
BackgroundPaper, Sand, Ink, Terracotta, OliveThe calm layer. Occupies the most area.
ContentInk, or Paper reversedAll type. Never Apricot.
AccentApricotThe one thing on the page that should be looked at.
The order cannot be shuffled. Apricot never becomes a background for a whole page, and Ink never becomes an accent. A palette fails most often by making its loudest colour the 60% — exhausting to look at, and it leaves the accent nowhere to go.
This resolves a conflict recorded in the brief. Safe wants quiet; act with me wants urgency. The resolution was always that the identity stays quiet and the call to action is the only loud thing in it. That is a 60/30/10 statement.

Forbidden pairings

PairingWhy it is banned
Light text on Apricot2.44:1. Illegible. On a good monitor the pair looks acceptable, which is exactly how it reaches print. Apricot is a fill and graphic colour, never an interactive one.
Apricot and Olive carrying meaning against each otherThey converge to 2.17:1 under protanopia — the same colour to a meaningful share of people. No status coding, no colour-coded categories across that pair.
Apricot on SandToo close in value. Turns to mud, and the accent stops being an accent.
Ink type on Olive4.12:1 — passes for large text only, and reads muddy. Use Paper on Olive.
Any blue, anywhereSix of seven childhood-cancer charities lead with blue; so do four of five pro-nuclear bodies. It is the most crowded hue in both categories this brand sits between.

Colour blindness

This changed the palette. In the first version Apricot and Olive sat at 1.22:1 under protanopia. Olive was darkened from #79876C to #4F5C46 so they separate by value, where colour vision cannot fail.
