Visual language · five

Photograph the treatment. Illustrate the fear.

One rule divides the two media, and it settles the tension the brief carried from the start.

Where the rule came from. The owner asked for "playful, cartoons-like — they should love it to understand it", and separately named UNICEF as the brand they admire. UNICEF runs both media and divides them by job: photography carries evidentiary weight for real events; illustration handles abstraction, identity protection, and anything addressed to a child rather than depicting one as evidence. Reconstructed from their published material — no official document states it in one sentence, so treat this as a working rule, not a citation.

The division

MediumCarriesNever used for
PhotographyProof that we were there. Volunteers, wards, families, events, hands, rooms.Explaining the science. Depicting fear or risk.
IllustrationThe science, the argument, anything abstract, anything addressed directly to a child.Standing in for something that actually happened.
If it happened, photograph it. If it is an idea, draw it. That single test resolves almost every case.

The four categories we shoot

CategoryQuantified rules
Presence
the core category		 An adult and a child, or an adult and a family, in the same frame. Available light only. Eye level or below — never shot down at a seated person. At least 60% of the frame is the two of them; the room is context, not subject.
Rooms Wards, corridors, waiting areas, gardens. No people. Daylight. Shot at standing eye height. Used for pacing between people pictures, never as the hero.
Hands and detail Held hands, a book being passed, a chair pulled up. Close, warm, shallow depth. The primary route where faces cannot be shown — and the one that always has consent.
Public life Festivals, stalls, schools, street. Crowds and gatherings. 100% candid — no posing, no looking at camera. Evening and overcast light are welcome; this is where the identity meets people who have never heard of us.
Mix across a set: roughly 50% Presence, 20% Hands, 20% Public life, 10% Rooms. A set that is all Presence reads as a campaign; a set that is all Rooms reads as an estate agent.

Treatment

ColourUnfiltered. Correct white balance toward warm. No duotones, no brand-colour overlays — a treated photograph stops being evidence.
CropGenerous. Room around people. Never a tight crop that isolates a face.
GradeLift the shadows slightly; hold highlights. Nothing should look clinical or contrasty.
Format4:5 and 3:2. Full bleed on covers; inset with Paper margin in text.

The dignity standard

Adopted from UNICEF's published photography ethics, which are not proprietary and are the right standard for this work.

Required
  • Informed consent from the family, recorded, for every identifiable child — and consent is revocable.
  • The child is doing something, not being done to. Active posture, agency in frame.
  • Where a face cannot be shown, use silhouette or hands.
  • Captions describe what is happening, with first names only where consented.
Forbidden absolutely
  • No pixelation and no black bars. Both mark a child as a victim. Silhouette instead.
  • No victim captioning. Never "a young cancer patient". A name, or nothing.
  • No medical procedures, cannulas, drips or equipment in focus.
  • No visibly distressed child. Not once, not for effect.
  • Avoid both stereotypes — the suffering child and the exploitatively cute child.
  • No stock photography of people. If we did not take it, it is not evidence.

Illustration

LineHand-drawn feel, even weight, rounded terminals. Never technical or diagrammatic.
ColourInk line on Paper or Sand. Apricot as the single accent; Olive for anything growing. Maximum three colours per illustration.
SubjectThe science, made physical — light through a hand, a seed opening, a scan revealing something. Re-skin the abstract as a familiar object; never draw an atom, a reactor or a trefoil.
PeopleMay be drawn as simplified figures without faces. No mascot, no recurring character — the owner excluded that, and a character dates faster than anything else in an identity.

Forbidden imagery — the whole list

Cooling towers, reactors, plant interiors, hard hats · the radiation trefoil in any colour · atom orbit-ellipses · sunbursts and radial rays · geiger counters, dosimeters, gauges · acid or glow green · hazard yellow with black · clinical steel-and-white · protest, placards, marches · dead or damaged organisms · stock scientists pointing at screens · staged smiling in hospital beds · any image using an ill child to extract sympathy.
Every item traces to a decision, not to taste. The trefoil is CRIIRAD's actual logo, recoloured purple — proof that recolouring does not neutralise it. The sunburst is the anti-nuclear movement's own emblem, in continuous use since 1975. The orbit-ellipse is used by three of the five pro-nuclear bodies audited. Using any of them puts this brand's name on someone else's argument.

Every photograph currently in circulation for Noyau is a placeholder. No free-licence library contains a volunteer sitting with a child in treatment — this was checked directly.
