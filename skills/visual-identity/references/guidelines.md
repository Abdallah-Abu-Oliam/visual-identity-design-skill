# The Guidelines Book

The final deliverable. **Its job is to make the identity survivable without us** — a
designer who has never met the brand should be able to work correctly from this document
alone.

Six core elements, always. Then optional sections, each with the trigger that decides
whether it belongs.

---

## The quality bar

A world-class guideline differs from an adequate one in four measurable ways. Apply these
to every page.

1. **Every rule carries its reason.** "Do not place the logo at the bottom" gets broken.
   "Do not place the logo at the bottom — the eye leaves the page there and the mark reads
   as a footnote" survives.
2. **Rules are shown, not described.** Eight wrong versions with a red ✗ beside the correct
   one beats any paragraph. People copy pictures.
3. **Specifications are measurable.** "40% men, 40% women, 20% children" can be checked;
   "authentic and warm" cannot. Wherever a rule can carry a number, give it one.
4. **Relative units, not fixed ones.** Clear space in X. Layout in percentages. Anything
   fixed breaks at the next size.

## Core — the six

### 1 · Brand essence

- Vision and values
- Brand personality
- Tone of voice
- Positioning / value proposition

**Use the "We are / We are not" format.** Coca-Cola's is the model — each voice pillar
states both sides: *"We are: direct, simple, active, bold. We are not: vague, complex,
passive, arrogant."* The negative half does more work than the positive; it is what a
writer actually checks against.

A voice spectrum — emotional↔dry, playful↔sarcastic, modest↔arrogant — gives the same
information as a slider, and pairs well with the Look & Feel axes already captured.

### 2 · Logo usage

The full anatomy. Founding Day's is the proven structure:

| Subsection | Fixes |
|------------|-------|
| Structure | What the mark is made of. The lockup is never altered |
| Colourways | Permitted colours per background, **ranked** so an unanticipated case still has an answer |
| Variations | Primary and secondary lockups, when each applies, whether parts may be separated |
| Clear space | **In X units**, derived from the mark itself |
| Minimum size | **Print in mm and digital in px**, both |
| Misuses | The don'ts, shown as images |
| Positioning | Where the mark sits on a layout |
| Co-branding | **Two rules, not one** — see below |
| Registered mark | If a ® or ™ applies |

**Co-branding splits in two.** Founding Day separates *sponsors* from *partners* and gives
them different rules — the primary mark sits right-side and 20% larger for sponsors, while
partner marks sit 20% smaller and follow the primary's alignment. Alignment rules also
differ for single versus multiple partners. If the brand will ever appear beside another
logo, both cases need stating.

**The registered mark scales with the application.** Coca-Cola specifies the ® at three
scales — signage, packaging, stationery — and notes that a small-scale wordmark needs a
1.5X symbol to stay legible. A legal requirement treated as a design variable.

### 3 · Colour

- Primary colours
- Secondary colours
- **Four codes per swatch** — HEX, RGB, CMYK, PMS
- Approved combinations
- **Forbidden pairings, each with its reason**
- Colour scheme layer order
- The logo on coloured backgrounds — correct and incorrect
- Colour rules for type
- Contrast and colour blindness

**Forbidden pairings are the part most guidelines omit.** Discord names each banned
combination and says why: *"too vibrant when combined, creating an uncomfortable reading
experience"*, *"too similar, don't create enough contrast."*

**Order the layers.** Discord assigns roles — decorative colour, text colour, background
colour — and states that the order cannot be shuffled. That is 60/30/10 made operational.

### 4 · Typography

- Primary face
- Secondary face
- Both scripts, set separately, if the brand is bilingual
- Hierarchy with real sizes and leading
- **Line-height varying by copy length**
- Measure — 50–75 characters
- **Fallbacks, always**
- Colour rules for type on each background

### 5 · Visual language

**Photography** — style, and the categories the brand actually shoots. Founding Day defines
four (portrait, landscape, events, historical), each with quantified rules: portraits are
black and white, chest up, eyes to camera, 40/40/20 across men, women and children; events
are 60% daytime, 40% night. It also specifies **image treatment** as a repeatable
procedure — remove background, apply noise, convert to black and white, optionally restore
one element in colour.

**Pattern** — the named element, with correct and incorrect cropping, and how it behaves
underneath content. See `pattern.md`.

**Icons and illustration** — style, weight, construction, and how they combine.

**Graphic elements** — any heritage or signature assets that are not the logo. Coca-Cola
carries three: the Contour Bottle (1915), the Red Disc (1930s), the Dynamic Ribbon (1969).
Each gets its own entry with origin and role.

### 6 · Applications

- Print
- Digital
- **Layout templates as percentage stacks** — Founding Day gives vertical, long vertical,
  square, square-ads, image-and-text, image-only and horizontal, each broken down
  (`04% margin · 08% logo · 17% margin · 19% text · 45.5% margin · 2.5% link · 04% margin`).
  Percentages mean one template works at any output size.

## Optional — include only when the trigger applies

Each of these appeared in a real guideline studied for this skill. **Do not include them by
default** — an unused section is padding, and padding is how a guideline stops being read.

| Section | Include when | Contains |
|---------|--------------|----------|
| **Strapline** | The brand has a tagline or verbal signature | Its own versions, colours and positioning rules — Founding Day treats it as a separate asset from the logo, with three subsections |
| **Sub-brands / product lines** | More than one line, edition or service exists | How each is marked without fragmenting the parent. Coca-Cola runs product and flavour palettes under one identity; Al-Nissal's brief names an "Edition" line and a sharpening service |
| **Motion identity** | Digital product, video, or any animated surface | Transitions, easing, loading, entrances, and what must never move |
| **UI style** | The brand has an app, site or product interface | Components, states, and interface-specific colour rules. Discord specifies user-status colours and server-channel treatment, and requires interface elements match the real product |
| **Iconography as its own section** | An icon set exists | The full set, construction rules, sizing, and whether icons combine into patterns |
| **3D assets** | 3D icons or objects are used | The set, and the rule that a flat variant remains the real asset |
| **Packaging** | Physical product | Structure, materials, print method, and how the mark survives the substrate |
| **Environmental / signage** | Physical locations | Storefront, interior, wayfinding, uniforms, scale rules |
| **Merchandise** | Gifts, stickers, apparel | What the mark may and may not appear on |
| **Seasonal / campaign extensions** | Recurring events or campaigns | Founding Day ships Ramadan decorations, a fashion guide and a culinary guide as separate sections off the main book |
| **Campaign history** | Established brand with a track record | Coca-Cola documents eleven campaigns with strategy and tone for each — it teaches the voice better than describing it |
| **Asset library** | Always worth including | Where the files live and download links per section. Both Discord and Founding Day put download links inside the relevant pages, not in an appendix |
| **Governance and contact** | Always worth including | Who approves exceptions, and where to ask. Discord ends with a single address; without one, every ambiguity becomes an invention |

## Two failure modes to design against

**Bilingual contradiction.** A national guideline shipped an edition whose Arabic forbade
placing the logo at the bottom while its English permitted it; the next edition corrected
it. **Every rule must be verified to say the same thing in both languages before shipping**
— translate-and-ship is exactly how that defect is produced.

**Placeholders left in.** One widely circulated brand manual names one typeface in its
heading while displaying another, and carries a swatch called "Caffeine Gold" whose value
reads `HEX #000000` and `RGB TBC`. **Before delivery, sweep the whole document for TBC,
lorem, unresolved values and contradictions.** A guideline that contradicts itself teaches
people to ignore it.

## Pre-delivery checklist

Run all five. The last two were found by shipping a real bundle and getting them wrong.

1. **Placeholder sweep** — TBC, lorem, `#000000` standing in for a real value, empty
   sections.
2. **Bilingual cross-check** — every rule verified to say the same thing in both languages.
3. **Contrast and colour blindness** — `scripts/palette.py`. Neither failure it catches is
   findable by looking.
4. **No named font without a source.** A token like `--font-mono: "Some Font"` that the
   page cannot actually load makes the design system report a missing brand font, and the
   deliverable arrives looking broken. **Use generic families unless the face genuinely
   loads** — `ui-monospace, Consolas, monospace`, not a specific name you have not embedded.
5. **The book must obey its own rules.** On one delivery the cover laid the mark and body
   copy straight onto the pattern — breaking two rules the book itself sets out inside.
   **Check the cover and every divider against the guideline's own pages.** A book that
   contradicts itself on page one teaches the reader to ignore all of it.
