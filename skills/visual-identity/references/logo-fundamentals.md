# What a Logo Is

Read this before generating anything. It governs `logo-types.md` and
`logo-generation.md` both.

---

## Three things that get confused

| | |
|---|---|
| **Logo** | The signature. An identifier, nothing more |
| **Brand identity** | The whole system the signature signs |
| **Reputation** | What people actually think. Earned, never controlled |

**A logo does not carry meaning. It collects it.** The swoosh meant nothing in 1971; it
means today what Nike spent fifty years putting into it. Apple's apple does not say
"computers".

Which is why **a logo that tries to explain the business is doing a job that was never its
own.**

---

## The failure to design against

**Asked for a knife brand's logo, a model draws a knife. Coffee shop, a bean. Bakery, a
wheat stalk.** Literal, descriptive, and wrong — and it is the default output unless
actively prevented.

Al-Nissal's mark is not a picture of a blade. Jamrah's is not a picture of an ember — it is
a folded geometric form that *feels* like one.

**Test every concept against this before presenting it.** If the mark is a picture of the
product, it is not a logo yet, it is an illustration.

---

## The three rules

Every effective logo satisfies all three.

### 1 · Appropriate

Fits the company and its field **through emotion, not description.** It does not say what
the work is.

Sharp, aggressive forms on a baby brand fail no matter how well drawn. Soft rounded forms
on a security firm fail the same way. Knowing how shapes produce feelings is the designer's
actual job here — and it is why brief element 7, targeted feelings, is captured as an
input.

### 2 · Distinctive

**Different from everything else in the field. This is the logo's primary function.**

And memorable in an operational sense: **see it once, redraw it from memory.** That is a
test, not a preference. Show it, take it away, ask them to draw it. If they cannot, it is
not distinctive enough to be remembered.

### 3 · Simple

---

## Rules 2 and 3 pull against each other. That tension is the craft.

```
too simple ───────── sweet spot ───────── too complex
generic, taken       redraw from memory    unrecognisable
already exists       and still unique      nobody retains it
```

A sheet of fifteen marks built from circles, triangles and squares demonstrates the
left-hand failure: every one is simple, six are a triangle inside a circle, and they are
interchangeable. **Simplicity pushed far enough lands on shapes everyone already owns —
rule 3 satisfied by destroying rule 2.**

Both ends fail memorability, for opposite reasons. One is forgotten because nothing
distinguishes it; the other because there is too much to hold.

---

## What this means in practice

- **Never generate a mark that depicts the product.**
- **Judge distinctiveness against the competitor audit**, not against taste. "Different"
  means different *from them*, specifically.
- **Run the redraw test** on the shortlist, out loud: describe the mark in one sentence
  someone could draw from. If the sentence needs three clauses, it is too complex.
- **Present concepts colourless**, so rule 2 is judged on form rather than rescued by
  colour.
- **Check the favicon strip** — a mark that dies at 16px fails rule 3 in the direction
  nobody notices until it is too late.
