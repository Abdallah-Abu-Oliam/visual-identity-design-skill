# Brand Questionnaire — Worked Example: Al-Nissal (النصال)

A knife and professional cutting-tool retailer in Iraq and the Arab world.

**Why this file exists.** It is not here to show what good answers look like. It is here
to show what *reading* looks like. Scattered through it are lines marked **→ Reading
note** — moments where a strategy answer silently decides something visual. That
extraction is Step 1 of the mood board process, demonstrated rather than described.

A model working through a real questionnaire should be producing notes like these
continuously. They are the bridge from what the brand *is* to how it *looks*.

---

## 1. Brand Strategy — Values, Vision, and Story

### What are your beliefs and values?

At Al-Nissal, we believe that:

- **Authenticity is a responsibility before it is a product.** Every item we offer must
  be genuine, verified, and thoroughly tested. We never sell anything we wouldn't
  confidently use ourselves.
- **Trust is built through details, not slogans.** The type of steel, heat treatment,
  packaging, and after-sales support are all part of our promise — not optional extras.
- **A tool is an extension of the hand that uses it.** A knife is more than a piece of
  steel; it is a tool for craftsmanship, livelihood, and precision. Respecting the tool
  means respecting the person who relies on it.
- **Quality is a non-negotiable principle.** We are not competing to offer the lowest
  price — we are committed to delivering the most reliable performance.
- **Sharing knowledge is our responsibility before making a sale.** We are a trusted
  source of guidance, not just a retailer. Our role is to educate the market about
  authentic products and help distinguish them from imitations.
- **Honest advice is part of who we are.** We recommend the product that truly fits each
  customer's needs — even if it isn't the most profitable option for us.

> **→ Reading note.** "Trust is built through details, not slogans" is a visual
> instruction disguised as a value. It forbids a loud identity. Craft, precision, and
> restraint over volume. Detail-level care in the mark itself.

### Why do you do what you do?

> "Empowering people to pursue excellence with the confidence of authenticity."

In practice:

- Restoring the value of authentic products in a market crowded with counterfeits.
- Protecting customers from fraud and unreliable tools that fail them in daily work.
- Raising the standards of the Iraqi and Arab markets by promoting appreciation for
  authentic knives and the craftsmanship behind them.
- Empowering butchers, chefs, hunters, homemakers, and enthusiasts with tools that match
  their passion and profession.

### What do you stand against?

- **Counterfeit products disguised as authentic.** Anything imitating the appearance of
  quality while lacking the substance.
- **The "cheap is all that matters" mindset** at the expense of safety and craftsmanship.
- **Empty marketing promises and exaggerated claims.** "The strongest ever," "cuts
  through anything." If we cannot test it, measure it, and stand behind it, we won't
  promise it.
- **Treating knives as weapons rather than tools** for craftsmanship and everyday life.
  We reject any messaging, imagery, or content that promotes violence or glorifies
  dangerous behavior.

  > **→ Reading note.** This is the single most decisive answer in the document. It
  > eliminates red from the palette — blood, danger, aggression. It rules out black
  > tactical aesthetics, sharp aggressive angles, fire and smoke. An entire visual
  > territory is closed by one value, before anyone has opened a color picker.

- **Disposable consumerism instead of products built to last.** Tools made to endure and
  be maintained — not replaced.

  > **→ Reading note.** Durability as a value argues for a timeless mark over a trendy
  > one. An identity that needs redesigning in three years contradicts the brand's own
  > stated belief.

### Why does the team enjoy working here?

- A clear identity: authenticity first, trust before profit, quality before expansion.
- They sell products they genuinely believe in — every item tested, standards defined.
- The environment is built around continuous learning: steel types, heat treatment,
  professional applications.
- They protect customers from counterfeits rather than simply moving inventory.
- They are never expected to exaggerate or chase short-term sales.

### Why do customers buy?

- **Trusted authenticity.** Genuine brands, transparent sourcing, documented specs —
  steel type, hardness (HRC), blade thickness, weight.
- **Performance proven through real use.** Testing, authentic content, professional
  reviews.
- **Expert guidance** instead of random sales advice.
- **Reliable warranty and after-sales support.**
- **A complete experience** — premium packaging, reliable delivery, care education.

> **→ Reading note.** Documented specs — HRC, thickness, weight — mean the identity must
> carry dense technical information gracefully. Implies a typeface with a real range of
> weights, tabular figures, and a layout system built for spec tables. This is a
> typography requirement extracted from a sales answer.

### Why do they stay?

- Products consistently deliver on the promises made.
- When a problem arises, they get a solution — not an excuse.
- The same honesty and service with every order.
- They feel part of a community that values authenticity and craftsmanship.

## 2. Vision Statement

### Next 5–10 years

- Become the leading reference in Iraq and the Arab world for authentic knives and
  professional knife-related equipment.
- Be recognized as the benchmark for authenticity and quality — not just as a retailer.
- Establish a trusted educational authority through expert content, workshops, and a
  specialized academy for culinary, hunting, and professional cutting tools.

### Plans for growth and expansion

- **A specialized platform** combining e-commerce, educational content, and community.

  > **→ Reading note.** The identity must live natively in a web application. Requires a
  > full UI-capable system: semantic colors, states, a type scale that survives at 14px,
  > components. Not a logo and two colors.

- **Flagship stores and brand corners** in major cities.

  > **→ Reading note.** The identity enters the physical world — storefronts, interiors,
  > signage, uniforms, packaging. Demands large-scale performance, material behavior,
  > and a single-color version that can be cut, embossed, or engraved.

- **Al-Nissal Edition product lines** with international manufacturers or to proprietary
  specification.

- **B2B partnerships** with restaurants, hotels, culinary institutes, and vocational
  training centers.

  > **→ Reading note.** Co-branding. Our mark will sit beside other logos and must hold
  > its own without clashing. Requires a clear lockup, defined clear-space rules, and a
  > horizontal variant.

### New markets

- **Phase One:** all of Iraq — Baghdad, Erbil, Basra, Sulaymaniyah, remaining governorates.
- **Phase Two:** selected Arab markets via the online store and a distributor network —
  the Gulf, Jordan, and others.

### New products and services

**Products:** signature Al-Nissal knife collections; maintenance tools (sharpening
stones, strops, cutting boards, accessories); accessories (sheaths, cases, professional
knife rolls).

**Services:** professional sharpening and maintenance; workshops and training for chefs,
butchers, and enthusiasts; membership and loyalty programs.

> **→ Reading note.** Multiple product lines and services mean the system needs a
> sub-brand strategy — how "Al-Nissal Edition" and "Sharpening Service" are marked
> without fragmenting the identity.

### Priority customer segments

Butchers and specialty butcher shops · professional chefs and restaurants · hunting and
camping enthusiasts · quality-conscious households · collectors and premium-tool
enthusiasts.

### How the company evolves

- From a retail store into a reference platform combining commerce, knowledge, service,
  and community.
- From a small team into a specialized organization — product management, content,
  training, after-sales support.
- An internal operating system translating authenticity, trust, and craftsmanship into
  documented, scalable processes.

## 3. Look & Feel

| Axis | Position |
|------|----------|
| Traditional ↔ Modern | **Modern with traditional roots** |
| Simple ↔ Complex | **Simple, minimalist, with careful attention to fine details** |
| Masculine ↔ Feminine | **Refined, understated masculine — while remaining inclusive and welcoming** |
| Premium ↔ Mass-market | **Accessible premium** — above mass market, without feeling luxurious or pretentious |

> **→ Reading note.** "Accessible premium" is a narrow band and easy to overshoot. It
> rules out gold, ornate serifs, and heavy luxury signalling in one direction, and
> discount-retail brightness in the other. "Modern with traditional roots" points at a
> contemporary form carrying a historical reference — not a vintage pastiche.

---

## 4. Tone of Voice

| Axis | Position |
|------|----------|
| Formal ↔ Informal | Professionally formal, yet approachable and never stiff |
| Playful ↔ Serious | Calm and serious, with subtle warmth when appropriate |
| Friendly ↔ Formal | Respectfully friendly; never loud, casual, or artificial |
| Bold ↔ Reserved | **Bold in facts and standards; calm and measured in delivery** |
| Direct ↔ Indirect | Clear, honest, straightforward — without complexity or harshness |

> Our voice is confident, precise, and knowledgeable. We explain things simply, respect
> our customers' intelligence, and let the quality of our work speak louder than words.

> **→ Reading note.** "Bold in facts, calm in delivery" translates almost directly into
> a layout rule: strong hierarchy and confident contrast in structure, quiet and
> restrained in decoration. Loud content, quiet container.

## 5. Value Proposition

> We help professional chefs, butchers, and knife and cooking enthusiasts across Iraq and
> the Arab world choose and use authentic, safe, high-performance tools that stand the
> test of time in both their professional and everyday lives.
>
> We do this through a specialized retail experience, expert guidance, and a
> comprehensive customer journey that includes product testing, authentication,
> documentation, warranty, and reliable after-sales support.

## 6. Photography Guidelines

**Visual style.** Premium, craftsmanship-driven, natural, minimalist, honest. Clean,
thoughtfully lit, free from exaggeration or clutter.

**Emotions to convey.** Trust · calmness and focus · craftsmanship and precision · a
subtle warmth (not cold, sterile, or clinical).

**Purpose, in order.**
1. Educate — explain the tool and how it is used.
2. Inspire — show the beauty of craftsmanship.
3. Promote — calmly and refined, clear offers without aggressive visual selling.

**Message at first glance.**

> "These are authentic, professional tools, captured through an expert lens, in a calm
> world of craftsmanship and precision — not flashy, aggressive, or dangerous knives."

**Required color palette.** None specified.

**Colors to avoid.** None specified by the client.

> **→ Reading note.** The client says no colors are excluded. The values section already
> excluded red by implication. When a stated answer and an implied constraint disagree,
> the implication usually wins — but it must be raised with the user rather than decided
> silently. Flag it, don't bury it.

**People in images.** Prefer no faces. Hands only, shown demonstrating or working with
the tools.

**Would damage the brand.** Blood or graphic butcher scenes · mess, dirty surfaces,
unclean kitchens · horror or weapons aesthetics (fire, smoke, skulls) · knives used
outside controlled culinary, craft, or responsible outdoor contexts.

**Unacceptable image examples.** Knives as weapons or objects of intimidation · graphic
or violent imagery · messy or unprofessional environments · overly dramatic effects,
dark horror aesthetics, aggressive compositions · showing off the knife rather than
demonstrating craft · anything cheap, exaggerated, or disconnected from professionalism.

**Admired photography styles.** Opinel · Benchmade · KnifeCenter · Böker · Arcos ·
Victorinox · Zwilling · Morakniv.

> **→ Reading note.** This is the most useful answer in the whole questionnaire for
> competitive positioning. These eight brands define the category's visual conventions —
> and therefore what is already taken. The differentiation work happens against this
> list.

**Top 10 representative images.**

1. A professional chef's hands using an authentic knife during precise preparation.
2. A craftsman's hands sharpening and maintaining a blade.
3. A detailed close-up of steel texture, blade finish, and craftsmanship.
4. A premium knife with natural materials — wood, stone, leather.
5. A professional kitchen with clean surfaces and controlled lighting.
6. A butcher working in a clean, respectful, skilled manner.
7. A hunter or outdoor enthusiast using a knife responsibly in a controlled environment.
8. A collection of professional knives organized neatly.
9. A close-up of product details — handle, engraving, edge, materials.
10. A calm lifestyle image of the relationship between user and tool: skill, trust, craft.

> **→ Reading note.** Wood, stone, and leather recur. Natural materials, warm neutrals,
> earth tones. Combined with steel, this points at a palette of warm neutral plus cool
> metallic — and it arrived from an image list, not a color question.

## 7. Visual Identity

**1. Primary language.** Arabic primary, English secondary.

> **→ Reading note.** Bilingual identity. The wordmark must be designed twice, in scripts
> with different rhythm, baseline behavior, and direction. Arabic leads. This is a
> foundational constraint, not a localization step at the end.

**2. Correct spelling.** Arabic: النصال — English: Alnisal

**3. Meaning and reason.**

**Al-Nissal (النِّصال)** — plural of *nassl* (نصل), the blade or edge of a knife or sword.

> **→ Reading note.** The meaning itself can generate logo concepts: multiple blades
> combined, a symbol built from knife edges. A name that is already a visual object is a
> gift — the mark can come from the word.

Chosen because it is an authentic Arabic word deeply connected to the field; short,
strong, and sharp in pronunciation and meaning; and directly represents the world of
knives without artificial branding.

**4. Logo concept.**

> "The authenticity people trust" + "the falcon that sees farther and strikes with
> precision."

Communicating: authenticity and trust · precision and expertise · strength with control,
not aggression · professional craftsmanship rather than a weapon-focused image.

> **→ Reading note.** Two metaphors are now in play — blades and a falcon — and they can
> compete. A mark trying to be both usually succeeds at neither. This needs resolving
> before concepts begin: pick the primary, or find the form where one contains the other.

**5. Slogan.** "Authenticity. Trust. Performance." — *not finalized.*

**6. Logo message or emotion.** Trust and reliability · authenticity and authority ·
quiet mastery (expert, not noisy) · controlled sharpness, not aggression.

**7–12. Likes and dislikes** — logos, typefaces, colors. *To be provided.*

> **→ Reading note.** Six blanks, and this is the normal case. The user knows what they
> like when they see it, not before. This is precisely what the mood board is for: it
> answers 7–12 visually instead of demanding they be answered verbally first.

**13. Required elements.**

- Logo design
- Colors and typography
- Official stationery — letterhead, business cards
- Packaging and printed materials
- Brand patterns and visual motifs
- Brand icons (optional)
- Social media assets — covers, profile visuals
- Photography style guidelines
- Brand identity guidelines
- Additional elements — stickers, gifts, promotional materials

Website design and development: a possible additional service *after* the visual
identity is complete.

**14. Preferred logo style.** Selected: **pictorial mark** (رمزي) · **wordmark** (نصي) ·
**combination mark** (مزيج).

> **→ Reading note.** Three selected where the question allows two. This is not a
> filling error to correct — it is the signal that taste is still unresolved, and it is
> exactly what question 7 exists to catch. Overflow on 14 makes 7 worth asking, and
> makes it answerable: *"you chose symbolic, wordmark, and combination — send a few
> logos you like so I can see which one actually pulls you."* Asked cold, question 7
> returns nothing. Asked here, it returns something.
>
> The three are at least coherent. A symbol, a drawn name, and the two locked together
> describe one system rather than three competing options — a combination mark that
> decomposes into its parts for different contexts.
>
> It also reinforces earlier answers. Co-branding needs a compact symbol. Physical
> signage and engraving need a mark that survives alone. A bilingual identity needs the
> wordmark drawn twice, in Arabic and Latin, both able to lock to the same symbol.
>
> Lettermark and mascot were rejected — consistent with "quiet mastery" and with
> refusing a weapon or character-driven image.
