# Brand Questionnaire — Template

The blank instrument. Six sections.

**How to use it.** Do not hand this over as a form and wait. Most people stall on a
blank document. The skill should default to filling it *with* the user — one section at
a time, drafting proposed answers from the brief and from whatever the user has already
said, then asking them to correct rather than compose. Reacting is easy. Generating from
nothing is not.

Hand over the blank file only when the user asks for it, or when they clearly prefer to
work offline.

**Answers are inputs, not deliverables.** Everything captured here exists to drive a
visual decision later. Tone of voice is collected because calm-and-measured produces a
different palette than loud-and-playful — not because this skill ships a voice guide.

---

## 1. Brand Strategy — Values, Vision, and Story

### Brand Values

- What are your beliefs and values?
- Why do you do what you do?
- What do you stand against?
- Ask your team: why do they enjoy working with you?
- Ask your customers: why do they buy from you?
- Why do they stay with you?

### Vision Statement

- What do you want to achieve over the next 5–10 years?
- What are your plans for growth and expansion?
- Will you enter new markets?
- Will you launch new products or services?
- Which customer segments will you focus on the most?
- How will your company evolve?

## 2. Look & Feel

Visual keywords describing how the brand should appear to its ideal customer.

- Traditional or modern?
- Simple or complex?
- Masculine or feminine?
- Premium or mass-market?

---

## 3. Tone of Voice

How the brand communicates.

- Formal or informal?
- Playful or serious?
- Friendly or formal?
- Bold or reserved?
- Direct or indirect?

---

## 4. Value Proposition

Complete the sentence:

> We help **[audience]** to **[outcome]** through **[how]**.

---

## 5. Photography Guidelines

- How would you describe the brand's visual style?
- What emotions do you want the images to convey?
- Should the images promote, educate, or inspire?
- What is the message the viewer should understand at first glance?
- Is there a fixed color palette that must appear?
- Are there colors that should be avoided because they do not fit the brand?
- Do you want people in the images or not?
- What would damage the brand identity if it appeared in an image?
- Are there examples of images that are not acceptable for the brand?
- Are there brands whose photography style you admire?
- What are the top 10 images that would represent the brand?

## 6. Visual Identity

1. What is the primary language?
2. What is the correct way to write the name in the selected language(s)?
3. What exactly does the name mean? Why was this name chosen specifically?
4. What concept should the logo focus on?
5. Will the logo include a short message or slogan? What is it?
6. What message or emotion should the logo communicate?
7. Which logos or brand identities have you liked?
8. What typefaces or fonts do you like?
9. Are there specific colors you want to use?
10. Which logos do you dislike?
11. Which colors do you dislike?
12. Which fonts or typefaces do you dislike?
13. What exactly are the required elements for the visual identity?
14. **Which logo style do you prefer? Choose two only.**

    Present this visually, with the example logos shown. The examples are the question —
    the labels alone do not communicate it.

    **Two only.** The limit is the point. Five checkmarks carry no information; two
    force a decision and still leave room for a primary and a fallback.

    **If they pick more than two, do not correct them.** Overflow is a reading, not a
    mistake: it says the category is decided but the taste inside it is not. Go straight
    to question 7 and make it specific — *"you chose symbolic, wordmark, and combination
    — send a few logos you like so I can see which one pulls you."*

### Note on questions 7–12

These ask for reference images and links, and many users arrive with none. They know
what they like when they see it, not before. Blanks here are normal, never a failure to
answer, and never a reason to block.

But do not treat them as dead weight either. **Question 14 and question 7 are a pair.**
14 gets the category cheaply, because anyone can point at Apple or Sony. When 14 comes
back overflowing or hesitant, 7 becomes both worth asking and possible to answer, since
it is now anchored to a choice the user already made rather than to nothing.

Whatever 7–12 still leaves open, the mood board answers visually.

### The pattern behind question 14

Do not ask users to generate. Ask them to choose.

A blank question returns a blank. A question with concrete, recognizable options
returns a real answer in seconds — and the answer is more reliable, because the user is
reacting to something real instead of describing something imagined.

Reach for this shape whenever a question keeps coming back empty. Named options,
recognizable examples, a hard limit on how many can be picked.

### Note on question 13

This one is a scope list, not a taste question. It defines what gets built and what
does not. Capture it precisely.
